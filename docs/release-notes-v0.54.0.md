# v0.54.0 – sale price, grid benefit, high-load calendars, electricity contract and forecast price sensors

Three new sensors show what you are paid for the electricity you sell, and what you pay and are paid in the coming days. The tariff settings gain the grid benefit (*nätnytta*) as a field of its own, the grid fee and the grid benefit each get high-load hours with a calendar of their own, you can say how your electricity is priced (quarter-hour, monthly or fixed price), and the export premium becomes what it should always have been, your electricity retailer's terms. The sensors are information only: they never affect the optimisation grade, the economics or anything the integration uploads. **It needs a Wolta server with API 0.119.0 or later.** An older server has no sale price: the sale sensors then stay at `unknown`, the purchase price works as before, and the new tariff fields and repairs do nothing.

- **New sensor: Sale price.** `sensor.wolta_sale_price` (English instances; `sensor.wolta_forsaljningspris` on Swedish ones) is the price you are paid for a sold kilowatt-hour in the current quarter-hour, in SEK/kWh **excluding VAT** (a private person does not charge VAT; the purchase price stays including VAT). It is the spot price plus the grid benefit of that quarter, plus your retailer's terms, plus the tax reduction for micro-production of the quarter's calendar year (none from 1 January 2026). The server works it out with the same function the grade uses for the export price, so the sensor and the grade cannot disagree. SE1–SE4 only, **disabled by default**.
- **Attributes.** `prices` of the sale sensors is a list of `{start, sale, spot, grid_benefit_peak}` points for today and, once published, tomorrow, and `prices` of the purchase sensors `{start, purchase, spot, grid_fee_peak}`. The flag says whether the quarter counted as a high-load quarter of that price's own calendar, i.e. which grid benefit (sale) or which grid fee (purchase) was used; it is `null` from a server that does not send it. `components` shows what the price is built from (`grid_benefit_ore`, `grid_benefit_peak_ore`, `export_extra_ore`, `export_extra_pct`, `export_credit_ore` per year, and the new tariff fields below), `reason` says why there is no price and `tomorrow_complete` whether tomorrow is fully published. `prices` is not stored by the recorder.
- **When there is no sale price, `reason` says what to do.** The state is then `unknown`, as on the purchase sensor, and the two prices are available independently of each other. `grid_benefit_not_set`: enter your grid benefit; `fx_missing`: Wolta has no exchange rate right now; `zone_not_supported`: the zone is not SE1–SE4.
- **New sensors: Purchase price with forecast and Sale price with forecast.** The same state as the plain sensors, but `prices` also carries Wolta's forecast for up to three days ahead (the days without a published price), with `forecast: true`/`false` on every point and `horizon` (1, 2 or 3) on forecast points, in time order and with no timestamp twice. They are separate sensors so that nobody reading `prices` on the plain sensors suddenly gets forecast points, as with *Spot price with forecast*. `forecast_days` lists date, horizon and issue time per forecast day, and `forecast_source` and `attribution` credit the forecast sources (`forecast_source` is `null` without forecast days). A forecast price is a rough shape of the coming days, not a price to rely on to the öre; the forecast never feeds the grade.
- **One request, only when you use it.** The four price sensors (purchase, sale and the two forecast variants) share one read of the Wolta server per 15-minute poll. With all four disabled (the default) nothing is requested. A failed poll keeps the last answer for as long as it covers the current quarter-hour, also when only its sale price (or only its purchase price) was available. The purchase sensor now also becomes unavailable when a kept answer without a purchase price stops covering the current quarter-hour.
- **Grid benefit fields.** Under Configure → Settings → Tariffs: *Grid benefit* (excluding VAT, öre/kWh; `0` is a valid answer; the field is named as on wolta.se), *Grid benefit, high-load hours* (optional, an exception to the former, so it needs it) and, for the percentage part, *Sold electricity, percent of spot* (the former export compensation percentage; in SE1–SE4 it is the percentage part of the grid benefit). The two amounts are shown in SE1–SE4 only and lie between −20 and 50 öre; the percentage is shown in every zone and lies between −100 and 100. A percentage needs its fixed part, as the grid fee percentage does, and the options form refuses the save and says so, both at the field and at the top of the form. A high-load amount without the *Grid benefit* amount is refused the same way.
- **High-load calendars.** The grid benefit and the grid fee (below) each have a calendar of their own, because an operator can use other hours and days for what it pays than for what it charges. A calendar is weekdays (Monday to Friday) from November to March between a *start hour* (0–23) and an *end hour* (1–24, after the start; the end hour itself is not high-load) in Swedish local time, minus the days its *holidays* rule exempts: *No holidays* (every weekday counts), *Public holidays* (New Year's Day, Epiphany, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Christmas Day and Boxing Day), *Public holidays, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve* or *Public holidays, Christmas Eve, New Year's Eve and Maundy Thursday*. The standard is 6, 22 and the third choice. A calendar is only used when its high-load amount is filled in. Choose the rule your operator's tariff describes. Months and weekdays are fixed, so an operator with other months, weekends or several periods a day is not covered.
- **Grid fee in high-load hours.** *Grid fee, high-load hours* (optional, excluding VAT, 0 to 500 öre/kWh, SE1–SE4) is for a time-of-use tariff: *Grid fee (transfer)* is then the fee for the rest of the time and is required too. The purchase price, the grade and the economy figures use the high-load fee in the quarters of the grid fee's calendar. The percentage of spot in the grid fee is always counted on the market's spot price.
- **Electricity contract.** *Electricity contract* is *Quarter-hour price* (the standard, as before), *Monthly price* or *Fixed price*. *Monthly price* is the plain average of the month's spot prices (quarter-hour from October 2025, hourly before); it is set after the month is over, so the purchase price sensor has no quarter-hour price then (`reason: monthly_price_contract`). Your retailer's cost for volume weighting is not part of the average: put it in the markup. *Fixed price* (excluding VAT, öre/kWh, the retailer's price including its markup); the purchase price is built on it instead of spot plus markup (the energy tax, the grid fee and VAT are added as usual); the markup is not used. *Monthly price* and *Fixed price* need *Contract valid from*: the grade and the economy figures use the contract from that date and quarter-hour prices before it, while payback, IRR and the purchase price always use the chosen contract. Wolta counts per quarter-hour, so a contract priced per hour should be entered as *Quarter-hour price* and can differ within the hour from what your retailer charges; contracts that mix fixed and spot prices are not supported. Changing the contract away from *Fixed price* or to *Quarter-hour price* removes the price or the date that no longer applies.
- **Forms and errors.** The new fields are named as on wolta.se (*Grid fee (transfer)*, *Grid fee, high-load hours*, *Grid benefit*, *Grid benefit, high-load hours*, *Electricity contract*). The options form says what is missing or wrong before it sends (a high-load fee without the other fee, hours out of order, a contract without its date or price) and shows each of the server's refusals at the field and at the top of the form, with the Tariffs section open. Re-authentication and linking carry the new fields too.
- **New repair: "Wolta: your high-load grid fee looks too high".** The same warning as for the grid fee, for *Grid fee, high-load hours*, with a limit of its own that the Wolta server sets (currently 80 öre per kWh), because a real time-of-use tariff reaches 61 öre excluding VAT. It clears itself once the server finds the fee plausible. Not raised in view-only mode.
- **Export premium is now your retailer's terms.** The field is now called *Retailer's terms for sold electricity*: what your electricity retailer pays (+) or deducts (−) per sold kWh on top of the spot price, `0` for plain spot. The formula is unchanged; only its meaning is, because many people entered their grid benefit there. *Export compensation, percent of spot* is now called *Sold electricity, percent of spot* and, in SE1–SE4, is the percentage part of the grid benefit.
- **New repair: "Wolta: separate your grid benefit from your retailer's terms".** Raised in SE1–SE4 when you have a retailer's-terms amount other than 0 and no grid benefit: Wolta adds a standard 5 öre of grid benefit on top, so a figure that was really your grid benefit is counted twice. Nothing changes until you save; the grade is calculated as before until then. It clears itself once the grid benefit is set.
- **New repair: "Wolta: your grid benefit percentage needs its fixed part".** Raised when you have a grid benefit percentage without the fixed part (Wolta would add a standard 5 öre as the fixed part, which looks like your figure but is a guess). It clears itself once the fixed part is entered. Neither repair is raised in view-only mode, where the tariff is set on wolta.se.
- **Both fields are saved together.** The form shows the grid benefit and the retailer's terms side by side, and saving a grid benefit also saves the retailer's terms (`0` if you left them empty), so that you have seen and confirmed both. The grid benefit fields are shown in SE1–SE4 only, and changes made on wolta.se show up in Home Assistant like the other tariff fields.
- **Translations.** The new sensors, fields, errors and repairs are in English and Swedish.
